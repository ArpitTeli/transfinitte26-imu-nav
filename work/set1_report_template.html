Verdict

The tuned filter generalises to an unseen session. Its set 1 error sits between the two training sessions on RMSE (10.8 m and 20.2 m there) and on drift per distance (0.18 % and 1.05 %). The test also exposed one real bug: a zero-velocity update can teleport the position by over a kilometre. In the full filter it caused only a single 6 m jump here, but it must be fixed before ZUPT is trusted.

Method

How the test was run

  • Frozen settings. All 13 filter settings came from frozen_settings.json, chosen by coordinate descent on the first 40 minutes of 2012-01-08 and 2012-02-04 only. No setting was changed after looking at set 1.
  • Per-session self-calibration from sensor data only. The converter fits track width (gyro yaw rate vs wheel-speed difference) and the magnetometer hard-iron offset (sphere fit), and averages the gyro bias over the still start. It never reads ground truth. The same step ran on the training sessions. Set 1 gave a track width of 0.589 m (training: 0.587 m, 0.598 m).
  • Wheel scale is frozen at 0.96, a value fitted against ground truth on the training sessions. It is not refitted for set 1.
  • Alignment. The filter starts at (0, 0) facing yaw 0. A rigid transform (rotation, optional mirror, translation) is fitted on the first 60 s of motion only and applied to the whole run. Later drift is therefore fully counted. The "ATE best-fit" column re-fits over the whole run, for comparison with other papers.
  • Error is the horizontal distance between the estimate and ground truth (NCLT's SLAM-derived pose), at every IMU sample (about 47 Hz, 320,000 samples).

Findings

What the test showed

The magnetometer is what makes it work

Adding it cuts RMSE from 228 m to 33 m and the final error from 670 m to 38 m. It does this even though 69 % of magnetometer samples were rejected as disturbed (95,942 accepted, 212,239 rejected). The 4-minute averaged heading reference and the weak per-sample weight let the accepted samples correct heading slowly without following local disturbances.

Bug: a zero-velocity update can teleport the position

At 37.0 minutes the robot stood still for about 2 s and ZUPT fired. A single update moved the estimated position by 1,290 m (a normal step is 0.03 m). The cause: the filter never receives a position measurement, so the uncertainty of x and y, and their correlation with velocity, grows without limit over a long drive. A very confident "velocity is zero" update then shifts x and y through that correlation. The same 2 s window also set the gyro bias to +0.00044 rad/s from noise, which drifts heading by about 1.5° per minute without the magnetometer.

The full filter was hit too, but mildly: ZUPT fired 273 times in it, and the largest position step was 6.1 m at 103.3 minutes (it happened to reduce the error, from 55.9 m to 45.0 m). The magnetometer keeps heading, and so the correlations, much tighter. ZUPT never fired on the training sessions, which is why tuning did not catch it.

Heading carries a steady +5° offset

The full filter's heading error is centred near +5° for most of the run (median absolute error 5.1°, 95th percentile 13.7°). A constant offset points to the magnetometer reference rather than to gyro drift, which would grow over time. Likely causes are a residual soft-iron error (only a hard-iron offset is calibrated) or a disturbed field during the 250 s reference window.

Wheel yaw-rate fusion still hurts

Turning it on raises RMSE from 33 m to 123 m and the final error from 38 m to 503 m. This matches the training sessions, where it was switched off. Wheel slip and pivot turns on the Segway make (vR − vL) / track width an unreliable yaw-rate signal.

Tuning was worth it

The untuned filter scores 119 m RMSE and 197 m final error on set 1. The frozen tuned settings bring that to 33 m and 38 m, a 3.6× and 5.2× improvement on data that played no part in choosing them.

IMU alone is not a position sensor

Double-integrating the accelerometer drifts to 17 km over 112 minutes. The IMU's job in this filter is heading (gyro) and tilt; position comes from the wheels.

Next steps

Recommended fixes (not applied)

These were found on set 1, so applying them and re-scoring set 1 would no longer be a clean test. Validate them on the training sessions and the simulator, then test on a new held-out session.

  1. Stop ZUPT and wheel updates from moving x and y. Treat position as a "consider" state in those updates (zero its rows of the Kalman gain). Position should only change through integration.
  2. Require a longer still window before ZUPT learns gyro bias, or learn it from the average over the whole still period instead of per sample.
  3. Calibrate the magnetometer's soft-iron error (ellipsoid fit, not just a sphere) to attack the +5° heading offset.
  4. Test on more sessions. Three sessions from one campus and one robot is a narrow base. A session from a different season or a different robot would show how far the settings carry.
